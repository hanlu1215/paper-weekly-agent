SEER: A Scalable Event-Driven Runtime for Parallel Execution of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Environments

  • 作者Jiming Su, Feng Zhu, Yuhao Xiao, Miao Zhang
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-11-05T13:24:57Z
  • 分类cs.DC, cs.MA
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is increasingly used in systems where many entities interact in a shared environment. In these workloads, policy inference can be batched on GPUs, but the CPU-side environment path still updates entity states, resolves local interactions, and builds observations inside each $\mathrm{env.step}$. This path can dominate latency in interaction-intensive workloads after policy batching. Existing vectorized environment interfaces and general event-driven runtimes do not directly address the fine-grained cross-entity work inside one bounded MARL step while preserving the step-level semantics expected by learning systems. We present SEER (Scalable Event-Driven Environment Runtime), a runtime for MARL environment execution under an explicit event-locality contract. SEER models each environment step as a bounded stream of timestamped events. If direct state updates are entity-local and cross-entity effects are represented as ordered future events, SEER can run independent events in parallel while preserving serial-equivalent boundary outputs. To make this contract practical, SEER uses a CAS-based dual-layer event queue: local event queues preserve per-entity causal order, while a parallel executive list exposes ready work to global worker threads. SEER further uses STPA, a preflight-guided thread-pool selection mechanism that combines short runtime traces with static workload and hardware features. We evaluate SEER on two parameterized MARL workloads: compute-intensive UAV swarm control (Aircraft) and communication-intensive wireless management (PCS). On Aircraft, SEER reduces inference latency by 16.2\% over the baseline. On PCS, SEER reduces environment-execution latency by 46.9\%, 19.5\%, and 64.9\% over ROSS-T, NBSE, and ROOT-Sim, respectively.

FlyingDrones: A Dataset and Benchmark for Optical Flow Estimation from UAV motion

  • 作者Laurenz Ruzicka, Roman Pflugfelder, Daniel Cremers
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-11-05T09:26:36Z
  • 分类cs.CV
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

Optical flow is an important cue for drone analysis in aerial surveillance, airspace monitoring, and long-range target tracking, where motion remains informative when appearance is weak. Estimating the motion of small Unmanned Aerial Vehicles, however, is difficult because distant targets occupy few pixels, have low contrast, and move against complex dynamic backgrounds. Standard optical-flow benchmarks emphasize large, object-centric motions and therefore do not characterize distant aerial targets well. We introduce FlyingDrones, a generated benchmark with dense ground-truth optical flow and segmentation masks derived from physically based rendering, and define resolution-normalized metrics (EPE Norm) that normalize flow by image dimensions. We evaluate 16 state-of-the-art model families on a motion- and size-stratified subset of 999 flow pairs at three resolutions. Every family recovers drone motion better than the zero-flow reference, but the gap between families widens with resolution: at 1920x1080, global-matching, recurrent, transformer, and short-window temporal models separate clearly from pyramid, classical, self-supervised, lightweight, and diffusion models. Within-family checkpoint spread is comparable to or larger than between-family spread, so the training curriculum is as important as the architecture. Two complementary studies test the benchmark beyond synthetic accuracy: a ground-truth-free motion-alignment evaluation on 21 cameras of real UAV footage shows that the synthetic ranking carries a moderate, significant signal about real-world performance (Spearman \r{ho} = 0.63 over 41 checkpoints) while the exact top ranks differ, and a fine-tuning study shows that 800 FlyingDrones pairs reduce held-out drone-region error by up to 37% and improve drone localization on the real footage for all four architectures tested.

**局限性：**
摘要显示验证以仿真与走廊实验为主，真实复杂室外场景的可行性尚不明确；标记投放属消耗性资源，墙面物理接触的安全性与可执行性未说明；触发指标的阈值与迟滞参数敏感性、长航时与多机协同情形，摘要均未提供足够信息判断。

**值得关注：**
该工作把“被动提取特征”转为“主动创造特征”，为弱纹理环境下的 SLAM/定位提供了新范式；其信息驱动、带迟滞的间歇干预策略，以及因子实验加消融的评估方式，对具身智能中的主动感知与无人机巡检研究均有借鉴价值。

Selecting the best UAV pursuer for minimum time moving target interception among obstacles

  • 作者M. M. Rizvi, Masuda Akter
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-11-05T00:55:50Z
  • 分类math.OC
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

A team of unmanned aerial vehicles (pursuers) with different positions, headings, speeds, minimum turning radii and launch delays must respond to a target that moves at constant velocity through a region containing static circular obstacles. Only one pursuer is launched, namely the one that can intercept the target first. We present a computationally efficient, obstacle-aware interceptor-selection framework for this setting. The interception problem of each pursuer is posed as a minimum-time optimal control problem and is solved over an explicit family of candidate path types through an arc-parameterised nonlinear programme in which the obstacles are toured tangentially and every part of the path is kept out of every obstacle by exact (not sampled) distance constraints. A closed-form lower bound, obtained by relaxing the curvature and obstacle constraints, is combined with the verified feasible interception times in a bound-and-select algorithm. We prove that the pruning is certified: a pursuer is skipped only if it cannot improve the best verified interception time, so bound-and-select returns the same pursuer and the same time as solving every pursuer, and the method reports a boundary on the optimal team interception time. Numerical experiments with solver diagnostics and repeated random starts, sensitivity studies in the target speed (with the selection crossover located by bisection), the launch delay, the turning radius and the obstacle configuration, a comparison with nearest-pursuer, fastest-pursuer and straight-line-time selection rules on random scenarios, a computational comparison with full enumeration for growing team sizes, and a geographic case study show that the selected pursuer is frequently neither the nearest nor the fastest, and that lower-bound pruning removes a large part of the computational effort without changing the decision.
