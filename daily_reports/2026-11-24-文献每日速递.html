A ROS2 based Human-In-The-Loop Framework for UAV Search and Rescue Operations

  • 作者Anubhav Pandey, Harshit Nagpal, Aditya Agarwal, Tejasvi Alladi, Fei Richard Yu
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-11-22T14:25:55Z
  • 分类cs.RO, cs.CV
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

Existing Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) autonomy frameworks lack human contextual awareness, limiting real-time adaptability in unstructured Search and Rescue (SAR) environments. To address data availability, we propose a Robot Operating System (ROS2)-based human-in-the-loop (HITL) framework that translates human directional gestures (left/right) into UAV navigation commands using RGB-Depth (RGB-D) sensing and YOLOv11 (You Only Look Once). The deprecation of ROS1 necessitates migrating to ROS2, prioritising real-time communication, scalability, and long-term maintainability. Unlike approaches optimised for peak accuracy under ideal conditions, the proposed system prioritises robustness and real-time deployment in degraded SAR environments. Our system achieves 89% confidence in depth estimation under occlusion and varying environmental conditions in Gazebo-PX4 simulations. In foggy conditions, RGB-D fusion consistently provides more informative cues than RGB alone, with an average improvement of 46.7% across all scenarios and distances. The framework demonstrates superior occlusion handling (+15%), adaptive RGB-D fusion, temporal consistency modelling, and real-time deployment via a ROS2 data distribution service (DDS), with end-to-end latency below 50 ms. We evaluate the modified YOLOv11 and the depth-weighted pose estimation on a held-out simulated test set and on real people from the public UAV-GESTURE dataset, in clear air and in Koschmieder fog. Without retraining, the framework reads the gestures of real people with 80% accuracy, depth keeps 77% at a visibility of 10 m where RGB drops to 24%, and the modified YOLOv11 reaches 91% in simulation and 99% on held-out real people when trained on real data. The open-source ROS2 implementation eases the transition of SAR-UAV platforms from ROS1, enabling UAVs to act directly on operator gestures without complex path planning.

Implementation and Software-in-the-Loop Evaluation of a Trans-Domain Digital Twin Architecture for Autonomous Guidance of the Phoenix VTOL UAV Without Online GNSS

  • 作者Mansoorali Amiri
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-11-22T02:30:36Z
  • 分类cs.RO, cs.SE, eess.SY
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

Autonomous UAV guidance in emergency response, infrastructure monitoring, and cargo transportation requires reliable navigation. However, satellite-based positioning may become unavailable or unreliable in enclosed spaces, blind spots caused by natural obstacles and structures, and environments affected by radio interference and signal spoofing during electronic warfare. This study designs, implements, and evaluates a system based on a Trans-Domain Digital Twin (TDDT) for autonomous guidance of the Phoenix VTOL UAV. Independent navigation, flight dynamics, and meteorological models are coordinated within a cycle of state estimation, prediction, decision-making, command execution, and feedback. Data from inertial, magnetic, pressure, and airspeed sensors, together with environmental information, form the basis for estimation and prediction. Furthermore, a shared state, temporal synchronization, preservation of information provenance, and command traceability establish continuity between models and decisions. The system was tested with a simulated physical twin in a software-in-the-loop environment. Vertical takeoff, movement toward the destination, landing, and autonomous stopping were recorded, and the dynamic model was adjusted to a limited extent based on sensor-derived feedback. Separating the evaluation reference from navigation inputs enabled independent assessment of positioning error and computational performance. The results confirm operational coordination, decision traceability, and closed-loop guidance under the tested conditions, providing a reproducible platform for the development and evaluation of navigation without satellite-based positioning.

Merits of Collocating Uptilted Antennas on Terrestrial Base Stations for Aerial Coverage

  • 作者Nesrine Cherif, Qurrat-Ul-Ain Nadeem
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-11-21T16:06:37Z
  • 分类eess.SP
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

The integration of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) into future wireless networks introduces challenges in ensuring reliable aerial coverage, particularly for UAV-based user equipment (UAV-UE). Traditional terrestrial base stations (BSs) with downtilted antennas, referred to as dt-BSs, are designed primarily for ground users. They provide inefficient connectivity to UAV-UE due to weak sidelobe gains, and susceptibility to ground reflection (GR) interference as well as sidelobe interference from non-serving dt-BSs. To address this, we propose a hybrid cellular architecture that collocates uptilted antennas on a portion of existing dt-BS towers, referred to as ut-BSs. These support UAV-UE exclusively, minimizing impact on ground users. We consider realistic antenna radiation patterns and probabilistic line-of-sight (LoS)/non-LoS (NLoS) links between ut-BSs and UAV-UE. Using stochastic geometry, we analyze the UAV-UE coverage probability, accounting for interference from both dt-BSs and non-serving ut-BSs, along with GR-induced interference. Results show significant improvements in UAV-UE coverage with only a small density of ut-BSs. GR interference is notable at lower altitudes but diminishes beyond 500 meters. This study highlights the potential of our hybrid architecture to enhance the reliability and scalability of UAV communications in large-scale deployments. It also offers actionable insights for telecom operators considering the collocation of uptilted antennas within existing infrastructure to better support aerial connectivity.

LoKiTO: Local Kinodynamic Trajectory Optimization for UAVs based on Memory-Efficient Neural Distance Fields

  • 作者Guillermo Gil-Garcia, J. A. Cobano, L. Merino, F. Caballero
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-11-21T00:24:35Z
  • 分类cs.RO
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

This paper introduces LoKiTO, a 3D local kinodynamic trajectory optimizer that combines a Chebyshev polynomial parameterization with an online neural Euclidean Signed Distance Field (ESDF) representation for aerial robotics. While current gradient-based planners often struggle with the high memory requirements of explicit volumetric maps or fail to strictly enforce dynamic limits, LoKiTO embeds kinodynamic constraints directly into a modular cost function and exploits the numerical conditioning properties of the Chebyshev basis. Collision awareness is provided by a continuous, memory-efficient neural ESDF updated online from LiDAR measurements, which drastically reduces map memory consumption to approximately 1.0 MB. Benchmarking in highly cluttered simulated environments against state-of-the-art baselines (Fast-Planner and EGO-Planner) demonstrates the superiority of our approach. Experimental results show that LoKiTO achieves over 99.4% dynamic feasibility compliance, significantly outperforming the baselines, and maintaining higher median clearance from obstacles. By coupling a well-conditioned continuous parameterization with explicit kinodynamic reasoning, LoKiTO provides a practical and extensible basis for local replanning in aerial robotics.
