FPV-CRASH: A Dataset for Offline Crash Moment Localization in Drone First-Person-View Videos

  • 作者Jifan Zhang, Tingyu Wang, Qian Liu, Zhedong Zheng
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-10-14T08:32:57Z
  • 分类cs.CV
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

Crash Moment Localization aims to localize the major physical impact that interrupts controlled flight in an onboard first-person-view (FPV) video recorded by a drone, thereby providing a temporal reference for retrospective crash analysis in aerial robotics. However, existing crash-video datasets predominantly target surveillance or dash-camera footage, and their annotation schemes and task formulations are not designed for temporal localization of the major physical impact in UAV onboard FPV videos. To address this gap, we present FPV-CRASH, a dataset and fixed-protocol reference evaluation for offline Crash Moment Localization in drone FPV videos. FPV-CRASH contains 1,052 drone FPV clips (526 Crash and 526 Normal); each Crash clip has a single-frame reference annotation for the Crash Moment, and every clip is paired with four English captions automatically generated from sampled frames and the known Crash/Normal label. Normal clips carry no timestamp target and provide training-time background supervision. The captions support an auxiliary Text-to-Video Retrieval task, whereas localization inference remains video-only. We further provide a heatmap-based reference model that estimates a continuous Crash Moment timestamp from sparsely sampled frames. Under the fixed, non-source-disjoint clip-level protocol, the reference model reduces MAE from 1.12 s for the strongest fixed timing-prior baseline to 0.56 s (a 50% relative reduction) and achieves a median absolute error (MedAE) of 0.33 s; the fraction of clips localized within 0.5 s rises from 37.14% to 65.71%. The release includes annotations, available source video identifiers and URLs, clipping metadata, fixed split lists, and preprocessing code so that the reportable portion can be reconstructed without redistributing source footage. At submission time, all source-compilation URLs remained accessible.

  • 作者Yanben Shen, Joscif Raigne, Asheesh Singh, Arti Singh
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-10-13T20:21:52Z
  • 分类cs.CV

Field pea has gained interest in the Midwest U.S. and has the potential to benefit Iowa cropping systems. This study evaluated 17 accessions and 7 Canadian varieties for agronomic and seed yield performance. This study found that PI accession trials exhibit genetic diversity in phenology and canopy traits, whereas the Canadian elite varieties demonstrated great yield potential under Iowa conditions. The accession genotypes matured between 75 DAP (late June) and 95 DAP (late July) across six site-years. The top-yielding Canadian varieties averaged 1961 kg/ha across two site-years. To comprehensively assess field pea performance, this study extracted time-series Unmanned Aerial Vehicle(UAV)-derived traits, assessed UAV-derived traits, and evaluated their correlations with multiple agronomic traits and yield. The results found that UAV-derived time-series canopy area was the most yield-relevant trait (r = 0.63) among all UAV-derived canopy features, vegetation indices, and manually collected measurements. Compared with yield, UAV-derived canopy area showed higher selection reliability (H2 = 0.87, CV = 13.46%), making it suitable for phenotypic selection and accelerating Midwest field pea breeding programs. This study identifies useful UAV-derived features for pea breeding and demonstrates the value of integrating diverse germplasm, elite commercial varieties, and time-series UAV phenotyping. Combining genetic variation from the accession trial with the yield potential of high-performing commercial varieties provides valuable breeding materials and phenotyping strategies for developing locally adapted, high-yielding field pea varieties for future production in the Midwest.

Collaborative Planning for UAV-UGV Teams under Operational Constraints via Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion

  • 作者Zihao Deng, Qianhuang Li, Maggie Wigness, John Rogers, Donghyun Kim, Hao Zhang
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-10-13T17:58:37Z
  • 分类cs.RO
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

Collaborative planning under operational constraints is essential for heterogeneous robot teams in large-scale missions. Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) provide rapid area coverage but are limited by onboard energy, while Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) can serve as mobile recharging stations yet are constrained by road-network traversability. Effective collaboration must enforce operational constraints during execution while enabling parallel, coordinated actions. Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) have shown promise through token unmasking but have two key limitations: entropy-guided scheduling assumes distributional alignment and reliance on differentiable constraints for diffusion-time optimization. We propose Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion (MEND), which formulates collaborative planning as a conditional masked generative process to generate context-aware action sequences for UAV-UGV teams. MEND introduces a neuro-symbolic diffusion paradigm that constrains conditional masked diffusion policies at execution by reweighting the learned action distribution with a logic-induced admissibility indicator, thereby enforcing compliance with operational constraints. MEND introduces an error-bounded inference scheduling at execution that constructs the unmasking schedule by selecting positions with minimal predictive entropy over the constraint-aware action distribution, enhancing collaborative planning optimality under operational constraints. Extensive experimental results in simulation, on real-world maps, and with physical robot teams demonstrate that MEND advances UAV-UGV collaborative planning under operational constraints.

  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-10-13T17:31:01Z
  • 分类cs.DC

Limited battery endurance remains a major obstacle to persistent UAV missions. Existing energy-replenishment approaches typically rely on battery-swapping/charging infrastructure or on ground vehicles with accessible road networks - assumptions that may not hold in infrastructure-less regions (ILRs) such as remote wilderness, mountainous terrain, and disaster-stricken areas. We formulate energy resupply in ILRs as a distinct logistics problem, in which energy itself must be projected into a region with no pre-existing infrastructure, and proposes a Distributed Energy Relay Framework that enables cooperative energy delivery using porter drones. The framework introduces a Transportation-by-Relay-and-Cache (TRC) mechanism, in which drones relay battery resources toward a mission location. Additional models are developed to characterize battery transportation capacity, relay amplification, energy-delivery capability, operational cost, and reliability. Based on these models, stations independently evaluate their delivery capability and submit bids to a mission coordinator, which selects an energy-delivery coalition that satisfies mission requirements while minimizing cost. Analytical evaluation illustrates the impact of battery characteristics and drone capabilities on energy projection and demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed framework for sustaining UAV missions in ILRs.

CALM: Decentralized Control of Cooperative Aerial Manipulation with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

  • 作者Jiaxi Huo, Petre Ricioppo, Fausto Francesco Lizzio
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-10-13T14:03:36Z
  • 分类cs.RO, cs.MA

Cooperative aerial manipulation is difficult under wind, payload motion, and noisy goal broadcasts because multiple Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are coupled through a shared payload. We present CALM (Cooperative Aerial Learned Manipulation), a decentralized controller for aerial manipulators carrying a basket while tracking a moving ground robot. CALM combines a parameter-shared Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) policy, trained using Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO), with goal estimation, per-UAV Extended State Observers (ESOs), basket-mass estimation, and anticipatory formation-yaw feedforward. The anticipatory formation-yaw feedforward uses estimated goal motion to rotate the formation before position error develops, reducing basket-yaw lag without adding net commanded force. A disturbance-conditioned critic supports centralized training while execution remains decentralized. Across held-out trajectories, CALM achieves lower mean position and orientation errors than all evaluated baselines, including the matched arm baseline (7.68 versus 10.95 cm and 3.3 versus 4.7$^\circ$), the cable-suspended realization, and two model-based controllers. Under unseen full-information-path degradation, CALM remains statistically comparable or superior to the arm baseline. Videos are available at https://petreric.github.io/CALM/.

**研究背景：**
空中移动操作要求无人机穿越室内、室外等异构环境，并在同一轨迹中连接导航与后续物理交互。现有空中基准多局限于单一环境，难以评估连续环境转换；涉及交互时，智能体又常被初始化在目标附近，导致从导航到交互的过渡未被充分考察。因此，论文希望建立长时程、交互导向的异构环境空中移动评测，推动空中具身智能走向统一移动与物理交互。

**研究思路：**
针对上述空白，作者提出 CrossFly 基准，在统一仿真与评测框架下汇集 15K 轨迹、20 多个重建场景，覆盖室内、室外及室内↔室外轨迹。方法上引入 Interaction Readiness Rate（IRR），不假设特定操作策略，评估终端导航状态是否为下游交互提供合适条件。实验使用五种代表性空中 VLA 智能体及 CrossFly-Agent，发现室内↔室外设置比单环境更难，未见场景最高成功率为 9.7%；下游操作案例进一步验证交互导向到达与后续物理交互的相关性。

**局限性：**
摘要未提供足够信息判断具体局限性。仅可看出评测基于统一仿真与重建场景，真实无人机系统验证摘要未说明；IRR 对下游交互的通用有效性目前主要由案例研究支持；跨更多环境、操作策略与真实扰动的泛化能力仍待验证。

**值得关注：**
该工作将“到达”重新定义为交互准备状态，为空中 VLA 与具身智能评测提供了新任务设定和指标思路。其室内外连续转换、长时程导航到操作衔接，以及不绑定具体操作策略的评价方式，值得后续研究借鉴，也提示需关注环境切换下的鲁棒性与导航—操作接口。

Compromised UAV reporters can poison latency telemetry and attract offloaded tasks to degraded servers. We present FleetGuard, an identity-capped conformal offloader for local, edge, and cloud execution. With equal buffers of m scores and a cap of K compromised identities, a Km rank correction retains one-sided fixed-level coverage without an honest-reporter majority. A deterministic repair scheduler allocates task-specific risk under fleet, capacity, and deadline constraints. Heavy-tailed simulations with eight reporters, four edges, 30 independent seeds, and 20 batches per seed and condition yield 2.08% deadline misses under seven low-score compromised reporters (95% clustered interval: 1.94-2.24%), compared with approximately 9.6% for pooled conformal, reporter-median, and mean rules. FleetGuard saves 22.3% energy over fixed-risk BRC and approaches adaptive exact assignment within 1.1%, with 2.6x lower mean scheduling time.

  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-10-13T01:36:45Z

A passive extremely large-scale reconfigurable intelligent surface (XL-RIS) offers a position-sensitive aperture for near-field sensing, but its element-wise signals are not directly observed. Recovering this virtual RIS observation (VOR) from fewer base-station measurements requires prior information. This paper investigates when calibrated propagation geometry or paired training data provides the more effective reconstruction prior. For a common sensing operator and target signal, we develop off-grid near-field sparse parametric reconstruction (OG-NF-SPR) and compare it with compact and DenseNet-based deep reconstructors. OG-NF-SPR uses reflected-path projection for position initialization, followed by Bayesian estimation of all path gains and continuous position refinement. A causal extension incorporates temporal correlation without trajectory labels. Controlled simulations use near-field UAV tracking as an illustration and separately examine noise, position-distribution shift, calibration error, and propagation mismatch. Under matched propagation at 20 dB, OG-NF-SPR achieves approximately 67% lower reconstruction NMSE than the two nominal deep networks. At low SNR, statistical averaging is more effective than selecting a single geometric explanation. Under sufficiently strong repeatable mismatch, a same-size network trained on the mismatch family outperforms the nominal physical estimator, whereas nominal training does not provide this benefit. Temporal regularization also reduces position RMSE from 0.653 m to 0.225 m on a shared trajectory. The results relate reconstruction performance to measurement quality, calibration accuracy, and training coverage, providing guidance on when geometry can replace supervised labels and when additional data are useful.

Learning Reachability-Based Safety Representations for Real-Time Robot Navigation in Unknown and Dynamic Environments

  • 作者Bojan Derajić
  • 来源arXiv
  • 发布时间2026-10-12T10:10:45Z
  • 分类cs.RO, cs.LG, eess.SY
  • 论文链接
  • PDF 链接

Safe robot navigation in unknown, dynamic environments requires more than geometric collision checking: a robot still far from an obstacle may be unable to avoid it because of its speed, orientation, or the obstacle's motion. Hamilton--Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis captures this in a value function whose sign separates states that admit a safe continuation from those where collision is unavoidable, but computing it online for every newly observed environment is too expensive. This dissertation trains neural implicit representations offline on reachability solutions to give optimization-based controllers this information in real time. Since the value function never exceeds the constraint, it equals a directly computed constraint, such as a signed distance, minus a nonnegative dynamics correction. Learning only this correction keeps represented obstacles outside the predicted safe set despite approximation errors. The first two of five methods use the learned function as a terminal constraint in model predictive control, for static local environments and predicted obstacle motion. The next two use it as a barrier candidate in control barrier function safety filters, for static environments and, through pairwise barriers in relative coordinates, for a varying number of moving obstacles. Simulations and hardware experiments on ground and aerial robots show better collision avoidance than geometric and hand-designed baselines, especially for short horizons and moving obstacles. The fifth method replaces the grid-based offline solver, which limits the first four to low-dimensional models. For control-affine dynamics with polytopic inputs, the HJ Hamiltonian is maximized at a vertex, so a parallelizable tree search over vertex inputs can generate training labels. With a loss from the HJ variational inequality, these labels yield barrier candidates for systems with up to 13 states.

In unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) networks, integrated communication and sensing (ISAC) is emerging as a key enabler to support extended and continuous communication and sensing capabilities for sixth-generation (6G) services among UAVs operating in swarms. In this paper, a comprehensive framework is presented to investigate the ISAC coverage probability of a reference UAV-base station (BS) in a UAV network. A fixed number of UAV user equipment (UAVUEs) and UAV-BSs are deployed in an aerial corridor modeled as a finite cylinder. The three-dimensional (3D) binomial point process (BPP) is used to model both the 3D spatial locations of the UAV-UEs and the UAV-BSs. By adopting realistic 3D third generation partnership project (3GPP) antenna patterns for both the UAV-UEs and UAV-BSs, the UAVs' boresight direction of the maximum antenna gain is modeled as a random unit vector. Consequently, considering both clutter and communication interference, exact analytical expressions for ISAC coverage probability are derived. Low complexity baseline analytical expressions are also derived by ignoring clutter interference and directional characteristics of the UAV-UEs. Finally, motivated by the vision for intelligent UAV networks, a DeepSets-based model is developed and trained to estimate the optimal location of the reference UAV-BS that maximizes ISAC performance given a spatial deployment of the UAVs. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to rigorously investigate ISAC performance in UAV networks while incorporating the directionality characteristics of the UAV antennas.

Launching a micro unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) from a hovering carrier and recovering it in flight requires reliable confirmation that the child is securely seated before the carrier is allowed to descend. This is challenging because vision provides relative guidance but does not directly indicate load transfer, and a downward-facing camera can lose its fiducial near touchdown. This letter presents a mothership-child quadrotor system in which mission transitions are gated by complementary nonvisual sensing. An upward-facing single-beam LiDAR on the recovery deck provides a binary occupancy state using hysteresis and a debounce interval derived from the child's occluding footprint and crossing speed; real-time kinematic (RTK) positioning conditions the carrier hover; and carrier thrust provides a proprioceptive indication of load transfer. In ten outdoor missions using an F450-class mothership and a Q250-class child with camera-guided recovery, nine completed successfully while RTK-fixed positioning was maintained through release and capture. A complementary MuJoCo study of fully autonomous fiducial-based recovery achieved touchdown 0.077 m from the deck center, while marker detection was lost within the final approximately 0.2 m above the docked configuration. In simulation, carrier thrust changed by -17.0 N at release and +17.3 N at capture, agreeing within 6% with the child's 18.0 N hover thrust. The results support a sensing architecture in which vision guides the approach, while range and proprioceptive information corroborate the final docking decision.

Camera-based occupancy prediction provides geometric and semantic scene understanding for robot perception and has been adopted in autonomous vehicle and UAV perception systems. Gaussian primitives offer a promising alternative by potentially representing only occupied regions, thereby concentrating computation and representational capacity where needed and reducing wasted computation and memory in empty space. However, existing Gaussian-based methods suffer from poor primitive utilization, as ineffective training objectives leave some Gaussians in empty space, while the limited expressiveness of Gaussian functions restricts their ability to model complex shapes. To address these limitations, we propose a novel 3D representation, Gaussian Neural Field (GNF), which can accurately map voxel positions to semantic occupancies. In GNF, Gaussians and a neural network jointly parameterize the field, where the Gaussians define its spatial support and provide features for neural decoding, while the neural network enables more expressive occupancy modeling than Gaussian functions alone. We further develop the Gaussian Neural Field Transformer (GNFormer), a streamlined and flexible method that predicts GNFs for camera-based occupancy prediction. Across Occ-ScanNet, SSCBench-KITTI-360, and nuScenes-SurroundOcc benchmarks, GNFormer achieves the highest IoU and mIoU and the fastest inference among the compared single-frame methods. In the experiments, GNFormer can represent indoor and outdoor scenes with only 256 and 1024 Gaussians, respectively, substantially fewer primitives than prior Gaussian-based methods. The code will be released.

Combining aerial robots with soft continuum manipulators promises safe, compliant interaction with the environment, but the coupled dynamics of a free-flying base and a highly deformable, underactuated arm are difficult to study without a suitable simulator. This paper describes an open-source simulation framework, built on the MuJoCo physics engine, for a quadrotor that carries a tendon-driven soft continuum arm underneath its body. The arm is modelled as a pseudo-rigid-body chain of twelve segments joined by pairs of orthogonal compliant hinges. Three tendons, spaced \SI{120}{\degree} apart and routed through every segment, bend it in any direction. A geometric tracking controller on $SE(3)$ flies the vehicle; its rotor allocation matrix is generated directly from the model description. A closed-form tendon-tension allocation keeps every tension within its limits and gives the same bending moment in every direction. With the nominal gains the vehicle tracks the mission with \SI{3.1}{\centi\meter} RMS position error while the arm tip swings up to \SI{37}{\centi\meter} from the body axis. The gain study shows that a low-bandwidth attitude loop lets the arm excite sustained oscillations. The simulation runs about $15\times$ faster than real time on a laptop CPU. All models, controllers and scripts that reproduce the figures are released.

In integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) systems, strict sensing requirements can substantially constrain the transmit power available for communication. A hybrid reconfigurable intelligent surface (HRIS) with passive reflection and active amplification capabilities mounted on an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) can enhance coverage and strengthen communication links in such power-limited regimes. This study evaluates and compares five convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures, AlexNet, Inception, LeNet, VGG16, and MobileNet, for diagnosing branch dieback (seca-dos-ponteiros) in pequi trees (Caryocar brasiliense).
